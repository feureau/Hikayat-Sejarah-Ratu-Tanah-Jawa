### **Part 2: The Javanese Pegon Text (Translated Summary)**


**[The Fall of Majapahit and Rise of Islam]**
This is the high history concerning the kings of the land of Java. It begins with the story of the connection between the older generation and the kings of Majapahit. It mentions **King Brawijaya** (the last King of Majapahit) and his Prime Minister **Gajah Mada**.

It details the internal conflicts and the eventual fall of Majapahit. It mentions the prophecy of "Sirna Ilang Kertaning Bumi" (Gone and lost is the prosperity of the world). It describes the transition from the "Budha" (Hindu-Buddhist) religion to Islam. It mentions the **Princess of Champa** (a Muslim princess married to Brawijaya) and the role of the Wali (saints) in converting the land.

**[The Lineage of Mataram]**
The text traces the lineage through **Raden Patah** (First Sultan of Demak) and **Raden Bondan Kejawan** (ancestor of the Mataram dynasty).
*   Raden Bondan Kejawan was entrusted to **Ki Ageng Tarub**.
*   The lineage continues: Ki Ageng Tarub -> Ki Ageng Sela -> Ki Ageng Pemanahan -> **Panembahan Senopati** (The first ruler of Mataram).
*   It continues through **Sultan Agung** (Anyakrakusuma) and the rulers of Kartasura and Surakarta.

**[The Java War and Diponegoro]**
The text shifts focus to the era of the Dutch (*Walandi/Kumpeni*). It specifically honors **Sultan Abdul Hamid Herucakra Kabirul Mukminin** (the title used by **Prince Diponegoro** during the Java War).
It describes his struggle as a *Prang Sabil* (Holy War) against the "infidels" (the Dutch) to restore the religion of Islam in the land of Java. It mentions that despite the war, it was the will of Allah that he was eventually exiled (referring to his capture and exile to Makassar).

**[Commentary on Culture and Wayang]**
The latter part of the text contains a lengthy treatise on the history of *Wayang* (shadow puppetry). The writer explains that in the old "Budha" times, Wayang was used for worship. However, in the Islamic era, the *Wali* (saints) modified the Wayang (creating *Wayang Kulit*) to be symbolic rather than idolatrous. The writer warns that treating these stories as absolute truth or worshipping them is forbidden (*haram*) in Islam, and they should be viewed merely as stories/history (*charita*).

**[Colophon / Closing]**
Written in the city of **Makassar**.
Dated: The 5th of the month of Ramadan, Year 'Be', Hijri Year 1303 (Approximately **1886 AD**).
Or Javanese Year: 1815.

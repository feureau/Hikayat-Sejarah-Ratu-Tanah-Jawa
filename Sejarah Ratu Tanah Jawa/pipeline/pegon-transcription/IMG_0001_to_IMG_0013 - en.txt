This text is a classical Southeast Asian Islamic manuscript—specifically a *Hikayat* (epic narrative)—written in the Javanese language using the Arabic/Pegon script. It narrates the Islamic tradition of the creation, fall, and earthly life of Prophet Adam and Mother Eve (Babu Hawa), up to the birth of Prophet Seth (Shith) and Prophet Enoch (Idris), and Adam's passing.

Here is the English translation:

***

**In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.**

All praise is due to Allah, the Lord of the Worlds, the Most Beneficent, the Most Merciful, Master of the Day of Judgment, and the ultimate good end is for the righteous.

This is the story of Prophet Adam, peace be upon him, as decreed by the Lord Allah, the Exalted, the One, the Subduer, regarding the story of the *Khuldi* tree (the tree of immortality). When Prophet Adam and Mother Eve were placed in Heaven, there was one tree they were forbidden to approach or eat from by the Lord of the Worlds. However, Prophet Adam and Eve were deeply tempted by the Khuldi tree because of the deception of Iblis (Satan). The Lord of the Worlds had explicitly forbidden Prophet Adam, saying, "Do not approach this tree, or you will be among the wrongdoers," and He warned them that whoever ate from it would lose their heavenly garments. Furthermore, the Lord of the Worlds sent the Angel Gabriel (Jibril) to remind Prophet Adam and to convey His strict command not to be deceived by Iblis.

However, great was the deceit and temptation. Prophet Adam and Eve eventually succumbed, feeling a profound sense of shame and regret toward Allah the Exalted once their heavenly garments vanished. Mother Eve told Prophet Adam that she had been approached by the cursed Iblis, who swore falsely by the name of Allah, deceiving them into believing they would live forever. Thus, the Lord of the Worlds became displeased with Adam and Eve. Their radiant, heavenly garments fell from their bodies, leaving them naked and full of sorrow.

Everything in Heaven wept for Prophet Adam and Eve. The creatures of Heaven sorrowfully pleaded with the Lord of the Worlds. Prophet Adam and Eve were in tremendous distress, wandering Heaven for days, crying out to the Lord of the Worlds, seeking forgiveness. There was an animal in Heaven, the Peacock (*Merak*), and the Serpent (*Naga*). Iblis used the Peacock to find a way, and he slipped into the mouth of the Serpent to bypass the angelic guards and sneak back into Heaven to whisper his deceit to Adam and Eve. Because of their complicity, the Lord of the Worlds decreed that the Peacock, the Serpent, and Iblis must also be expelled from Heaven.

Then, Allah the Exalted commanded the Angel Gabriel, peace be upon him, to descend and expel Prophet Adam, Eve, the cursed Iblis, the Peacock, and the Serpent from Heaven to the Earthly realm. Because Iblis had caused Adam to sin, the three of them were cast down by the Lord of the Worlds from Heaven to Earth. This was the decree of Allah, the One, the Subduer. Prophet Adam was commanded to leave the presence of the Lord of the Worlds and seek forgiveness.

When the Angel Gabriel expelled them from Heaven by the command of Allah, he took Prophet Adam by his right hand and Eve by his left, bringing them down to Earth. They were separated. Prophet Adam fell upon Mount Serandib in the land of Ceylon (Sri Lanka). Mother Eve fell upon Mount Arafat in the land of Hijaz/Syam. The Serpent fell in the land of Yemen, the Peacock fell in the land of Java, and the Angel Gabriel cast the cursed Iblis into the barren lands of Isfahan. Iblis was overjoyed that Adam had been cast out of Heaven, thinking he had won, though he too was banished to the Earth.

As for Prophet Adam, he underwent severe penance for his sins. Stranded on Mount Serandib in Ceylon, he cried day and night, his tears flowing ceaselessly as he deeply repented and begged for forgiveness from Allah, the One, the Subduer. So immense was his sorrow and the volume of his tears that they formed springs and rivers. Likewise, Mother Eve on Mount Arafat wept day and night, entirely devoted to seeking repentance from the Lord of the Worlds, her tears also forming pools and streams. The Serpent in Yemen also suffered, stripped of its legs. As for the Peacock that fell in the land of Java, its punishment was to bear beautiful feathers but to have an ugly voice, serving as a reminder of its part in the deception.

The cursed Iblis, because of his deceitful nature, became the eternal enemy of Prophet Adam and his descendants. Iblis had hidden inside the Serpent to bypass the guards of Heaven. He had spent years trying to find a way in, and by doing so, caused the downfall of mankind.

After enduring immense sorrow and crying until he was weak, Prophet Adam’s repentance was finally accepted by the Lord of the Worlds. For he truly humbled himself, surrendered his soul, and severed all ties with the cursed Iblis, seeking refuge only in Allah. For three hundred years, Prophet Adam and Mother Eve wept in sincere repentance to Allah, the One, the Subduer, the Mighty and Majestic.

Then, the forgiveness of Allah came to Prophet Adam and Mother Eve. The Angel Gabriel descended to Earth, bringing the mercy of Allah and bringing garments from Heaven for Adam and Eve. Gabriel arrived bearing the message that their repentance was accepted, provided that Adam and his descendants remain steadfast on Earth until the time comes when they are permitted to return to Heaven. Hearing this, Prophet Adam fell into prostration, weeping in gratitude to the command of Allah. Gabriel then guided Prophet Adam from Mount Serandib in Ceylon using a staff made of heavenly wood.

It is said that during his time of penance on Mount Serandib, Adam would pray the morning prayer (Fajr) and prostrate so deeply and for so long that his tears soaked the stones. As the sun rose, Gabriel came to him, offering greetings of peace. Adam stood, shook Gabriel's hand, and Gabriel delivered the glad tidings: Allah had forgiven him. Gabriel informed him that he would live on Earth for a designated time (about a thousand years), after which he would return to the mercy of Allah. Gabriel then brought him garments and instructed him to go and reunite with Mother Eve.

Prophet Adam traveled from Mount Serandib holding his heavenly staff. Meanwhile, Mother Eve was at Mount Arafat. Gabriel also came to her, bringing the good news of her forgiveness and giving her heavenly garments. After she prostrated in immense gratitude, Gabriel guided her.

Prophet Adam and Mother Eve finally reunited at the site of the Masjid al-Haram (in Mecca). When they saw each other in the distance, adorned once again in garments of grace, Mother Eve ran to Prophet Adam, and they embraced inside the boundaries of the Masjid al-Haram, shedding tears of joy. After their reunion, they prostrated together to Allah, the One, the Subduer. Then Gabriel came, conveying Allah’s command that Adam would be the Caliph (leader/steward) on Earth. He was given ten scrolls (Sahifas) of revelation, agricultural tools, seeds from Heaven, a male and female horse, and a male and female sheep.

The heavenly seeds were planted, sprouting into the various crops and plants of the Earth. As Prophet Adam and Mother Eve lived their lives on Earth, Allah granted them knowledge of His attributes, for Adam was His Caliph. Adam taught this truth to his wife and to the children that would follow. Gabriel also taught Adam how to build and craft the necessities of life on Earth.

Mother Eve gave birth to many children. Every pregnancy resulted in twins—a boy and a girl. Among the first was Qabil (Cain) and his twin sister, and later Habil (Abel) and his twin sister. According to the decree of Prophet Adam, the twins were to cross-marry (Qabil with Habil’s twin, and Habil with Qabil’s twin). However, a dispute arose because Qabil's twin was more beautiful, and he refused the arrangement. This led to jealousy, the temptation of Iblis, and ultimately a great tragedy between the brothers.

Following the tragedy and the dispersal of some of his rebellious children, Prophet Adam and Mother Eve were engulfed in grief. They prayed to Allah for a righteous child. Their prayers were answered, and Mother Eve gave birth to a single, pure son without a twin. He was named Prophet Shith (Seth). He was a comfort to them and obedient to the commands of Allah and his father.

As time passed, those of Adam's children who rebelled wandered to distant lands, some falling deeply into the deception of Iblis and becoming the ancestors of disbelievers and wrongdoers. However, those who followed Prophet Adam remained upon the truth.

When Prophet Seth reached adulthood, he received fifty scrolls (Sahifas) of revelation from Allah. Prophet Adam and Eve worried about his marriage, as he had no twin sister to marry. In His mercy, Allah sent Gabriel down from Heaven bringing a heavenly maiden (Bidadari) named Dewi Shujairah. Gabriel officiated their marriage at the Masjid al-Haram. From Prophet Seth and Dewi Shujairah came a noble lineage, leading eventually to a righteous descendant named Prophet Idris (Enoch). Prophet Idris was later granted a Book (revelations) by the Lord of the Worlds.

By this time, Prophet Adam, the Caliph of Allah on Earth, had lived for 950 years (a thousand less fifty). His time had come. The Lord of the Worlds called him back. The Angel Gabriel descended once more to gently take his soul, returning him to the mercy of Allah in Heaven. Not long after, Mother Eve passed away, returning to the mercy of Allah. Prophet Seth then took on the mantle of leadership, becoming the Caliph to guide humanity on Earth by the command of the Lord Allah, the Exalted, the One, the Subduer.

*Allah forgives and knows best.*

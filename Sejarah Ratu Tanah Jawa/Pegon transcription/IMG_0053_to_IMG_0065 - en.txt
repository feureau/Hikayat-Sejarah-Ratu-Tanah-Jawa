Then spoke Prabu Banjaransari, gently addressing the matter, conveying his heart’s desire for the woman who possessed such great beauty. He commanded, "Go forth, you whom I name Suradiwati, take this message to the Queen. Tell her that I send my greetings and that I have a great longing for her. If she accepts, then all is well. But if she refuses my proposal and chooses to resist, tell her clearly that I will not hesitate. However, say that it would be a pity for her people if war were to break out. Speak effectively, Suradiwati, and persuade her, for I truly desire to unite with her."

Suradiwati, having received the command, departed immediately, walking quickly without stopping until she arrived at the destination. She sought an audience with the Queen, Prabu Wanodia. When she was admitted, Suradiwati sat respectfully and spoke, "Your Highness, I am sent as an envoy by Prabu Banjaransari. He sends his utmost regards and wishes to convey that he is deeply enamored with you. He asks for your hand. If you accept, he will treat you with the greatest honor. However, he also says that if you refuse, he is prepared to use force, though he wishes to spare your people from the ravages of war."

Upon hearing this, Prabu Wanodia’s face turned red with anger. She snapped, "How dare he! Does he think I am a woman who can be easily intimidated? Tell your master that I, Prabu Wanodia, am not afraid. I will not surrender to his threats. If he wants me, he must face my army first. I would rather die in battle than submit to such arrogance!" She then ordered her commanders and troops to prepare for war, commanding the gongs to be beaten and the banners raised. Suradiwati, seeing the Queen’s fury and determination, bowed and excused herself to return to Prabu Banjaransari.

Suradiwati returned and reported everything to Prabu Banjaransari—the Queen’s anger, her refusal, and the mobilization of her army. Prabu Banjaransari smiled and said, "If that is her will, then so be it. But I do not wish to slaughter innocent humans." He then summoned his spiritual ally, the King of the Spirits (Ratu Jim/Sindul). "Uncle," said Banjaransari, "The Queen chooses war. But I pity her human soldiers. Therefore, summon your armies—the Djinns, the Peris, the spirits, and the ghosts. Let them march to the battlefield. Let them terrify the enemy, but tell them not to kill unless necessary. I want to conquer her pride, not destroy her kingdom."

The King of Spirits obeyed. Suddenly, the sky darkened as thousands of supernatural beings materialized. There were loud, terrifying noises, booming voices, and eerie shrieks. The army of spirits marched towards Prabu Wanodia’s kingdom. When the Queen’s human soldiers saw the approaching horde of demons, monsters, and spirits of all shapes and sizes, their courage shattered. They could fight men, but they could not fight shadows and nightmares. Panic spread through the ranks.

The Queen’s commanders tried to rally the troops, but it was useless. The horses reared in terror, and the soldiers threw down their weapons and fled in every direction. Prabu Wanodia watched in horror as her defenses crumbled without a single distinct battle being fought. Suradiwati approached the Queen again amidst the chaos and said, "Your Highness, please look. Resistance is futile. These are not human enemies; they are the forces of the unseen. Prabu Banjaransari possesses power beyond mere mortals. For the sake of your safety and your kingdom, you must yield."

Realizing she was completely outmatched and that her human army could not stand against the supernatural forces, Prabu Wanodia’s pride broke. She saw that Prabu Banjaransari had conquered her not with bloodshed, but with an overwhelming display of power. With a heavy heart, she signaled her surrender, allowing Prabu Banjaransari to enter her palace, not as a destroyer, but as a victor who had claimed her realm.

## Folio 165 (Visual Right)

1. and the sons of the king sat together in attendance in the presence of their royal father,  
2. listening to his counsel regarding the governance of the realm and the steadfast preservation of righteousness.  
3. At that time, the prince Raden Patah, who was born of the Chinese princess,  
4. had taken up residence in the forest of Glagah Wangi, which was later named Demak Bintara.  
5. By the decree of the sovereign, he was invested as ruler and given dominion over the coastal territories,  
6. while remaining obedient and paying customary homage to the throne of Majapahit.  
7. All the grandees and noble lords acknowledged his wisdom, valour, and devout uprightness,  
8. and people from diverse lands gathered to settle within his growing domain.  
9. Great prosperity attended the realm of Demak, and ships frequented its bustling ports,  
10. bringing abundant merchandise and the teachings of the Islamic faith across the seas.  
11. In Majapahit, King Brawijaya observed the flourishing state of his royal son's province with goodwill,  
12. recognizing that the destined shift of authority ordained by the Almighty was steadily drawing near.  
13. The ministers and venerable counselors reflected upon the ancient prophecies inscribed in the chronicles,  
14. which foretold that after the passing of four decades, the royal majesty of Majapahit  
15. would yield its paramount dominion to the rising power established upon the northern shores.  
16. Thus the king commanded that all registers of lands, heirlooms, and titles be faithfully ordered,  
17. so that no discord might arise among the brethren when the hour of succession arrived.

## Folio 166 (Visual Left)

1. When the appointed season arrived, the holy saints and teachers of religion assembled in council,  
2. discussing the establishment of a sacred sanctuary wherein the people might worship in unity.  
3. Sultan Senapati Jimbun, also known as Raden Patah, gave royal patronage and resources,  
4. and the venerable Sunan Giri, together with Sunan Bonang and Sunan Kalijaga, laid the foundation  
5. of the Great Mosque of Demak, setting up the great pillars of timber gathered from the royal forests.  
6. All the folk, both high and low, laboured joyfully in the construction of the sacred house of prayer,  
7. which shone with great magnificence as a beacon of the true path throughout the island of Java.  
8. In those days, embassies were sent to the capital of Majapahit with tokens of respect and filial devotion,  
9. reassuring the aged sovereign of Demak's fidelity and peaceful intent toward the ancient seat of kings.  
10. King Brawijaya received the envoys with royal graciousness and bestowed upon them rich gifts of cloth and gold,  
11. affirming that the ties of blood between the house of Majapahit and the house of Demak remained unbroken.  
12. Nevertheless, certain courtiers and provincial lords harboured doubts within their hearts,  
13. whispering of impending war and urging the mobilization of troops along the western borders.  
14. But the sovereign rebuked those who sowed dissension, declaring that what was decreed by Heaven  
15. could not be overturned by mortal weapons, and that peace was the greatest treasure of a realm.  
16. Thus tranquil order prevailed across the plains and coastal havens of the land,  
17. while the hearts of men turned increasingly toward the sublime teachings of the new era.

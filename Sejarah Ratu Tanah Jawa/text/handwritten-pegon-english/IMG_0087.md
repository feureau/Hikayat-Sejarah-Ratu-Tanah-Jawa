# Sejarah Ratu Tanah Jawa — IMG_0087

## Folio 173 (Visual Right)

1. Indeed, all of them in India were subject to their sovereign kings,  
2. embracing Islam, as well as the Jews and Christians, whose kings  
3. followed the royal commands, obeying the established governance in foreign lands.  
4. Likewise, the people of Rum, arriving upon the shores of the land  
5. of Java, found that its inhabitants were of diverse customs, paying homage to their rulers.  
6. The ancient kings were venerated, yet when Buddhism prevailed, it became the general religion  
7. of the people, who worshipped deities and celestial spirits, while the common folk  
8. followed the customs of the land of Java, inhabiting coastal settlements,  
9. and worshipping ancestral spirits, so that the people lived according to their own traditions.  
10. The people of Java and the people of Rum met upon the trading shores,  
11. and since the earliest times of the ancient kings, the lineages were recorded in chronicles,  
12. recounting the tales of deities, gods, and sacred entities who descended upon the earth,  
13. who were venerated as guardian spirits, possessing supernatural knowledge and celestial powers.  
14. Those teachings were learned by the inhabitants along the seacoasts, and many people  
15. of Java were called Buddhists, while the island of Java remained in an era of ancient darkness.  
16. Then arose the rulers of Majapahit, where the Buddhist ways were practiced by the inhabitants of Java.  
17. Finally, thus ends the record of the ancient chronicles of the kings of Java.

## Folio 174 (Visual Left)

1. Buddhism was established, and in Majapahit it became the story that  
2. was told and commemorated. The chronicle begins from the origins in Majapahit,  
3. continuing to Pajajaran, and so it became the narrative  
4. of the land of Java, handed down through generations among the people  
5. of Java, from the initial settlements until the emergence of kings.  
6. The kings of Pajajaran and Majapahit established their rule and customs,  
7. which were followed by the people of Java. Thus begins the chronicle of  
8. Pajajaran, starting from the beginning of the world, recounting the origin of the gods,  
9. the spirits and divine entities, down to the appearance of mortal mankind.  
10. In Pajajaran, from the time of Prince Mundingsari down to Prabu Siliwangi,  
11. and continuing down through the lineages recorded in the ancient writings,  
12. it is written that the people worshipped ancestral deities,  
13. and venerated nature spirits because there were human souls  
14. in the land of Java. Then Sultan of Rum sent an emissary  
15. of people from Rum to inhabit and cultivate the land of Java,  
16. and those emissaries, by the command of the Sultan of Rum,  
17. arrived to settle the uninhabited wilderness of the island of Java.

## Folio 153 (Visual Right)

1. Kyai Patih Gajah Mada was then dispatched as an envoy to Pajajaran,  
2. accompanied by Pangeran Lowan and a stately retinue of nobles;  
3. the journey was undertaken to deliver royal letters of alliance,  
4. proposing peaceful relations and marital ties between the royal houses.  
5. They traveled through mountain routes and coastal plains with banners flying,  
6. escorted by trusted palace guards armed with lances and shields;  
7. upon arriving at the borders of Pajajaran, they were met by regional chiefs,  
8. who received the emissaries of Majapahit with formal honors and hospitality.  
9. Kyai Patih Gajah Mada was granted audience before the King of Pajajaran,  
10. presenting the royal message and precious gifts of gold and woven silks;  
11. the King of Pajajaran received the proposal with measured deliberation,  
12. consulting his council of ministers and high priests regarding the alliance.  
13. The ministers weighed the advantages of eternal amity between both kingdoms,  
14. noting that peace would bring security and shared prosperity to all of Java;  
15. while Gajah Mada spoke with eloquent diplomacy, assuring mutual respect and honor,  
16. winning the trust of many advisors present in the council chamber;  
17. whereupon the King consented to prepare the royal retinue for the journey.

## Folio 154 (Visual Left)

1. Now the preparations having commenced, news was brought to the court  
2. that the royal procession of Pajajaran was ready to set forth towards Majapahit;  
3. the princess was accompanied by her noble parents and a distinguished escort,  
4. including royal ministers, senior commanders, and handmaidens bearing jewels.  
5. They encamped at the royal field of Bubat on the outskirts of Majapahit,  
6. awaiting the formal ceremonial reception by Prabu Brawijaya and his court;  
7. however, misinterpretations and conflicting intentions arose regarding protocol,  
8. as Gajah Mada maintained that the princess should be presented as tribute,  
9. whereas the king and nobles of Pajajaran regarded the union as an alliance of equals.  
10. Tensions escalated rapidly between the commanders on both sides,  
11. and pride prevented either party from yielding ground in the dispute;  
12. tragic conflict erupted upon the plains of Bubat between the hosts,  
13. in which the warriors of Pajajaran fought with indomitable valor to the end,  
14. choosing death before dishonor in defense of their sovereign's dignity.  
15. The news of the calamity brought profound sorrow to King Brawijaya,  
16. who mourned the tragic outcome and rebuked Gajah Mada for his rigid pride;  
17. and the memory of Bubat remained an enduring sorrow in the history of Java.

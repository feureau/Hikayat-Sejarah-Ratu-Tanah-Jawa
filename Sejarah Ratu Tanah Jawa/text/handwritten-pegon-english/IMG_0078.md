## Folio 155 (Visual Right)

1. Now after the tragic events of Bubat, King Brawijaya  
2. observed deep mourning and penance within the palace of Majapahit;  
3. he withdrew from public spectacles, dedicating his hours to prayer,  
4. repenting of the loss of life and seeking spiritual peace for the realm.  
5. The royal priests performed purification rites throughout the capital,  
6. cleansing the sacred grounds and offering prayers for harmony and renewal;  
7. meanwhile, Gajah Mada retired to his estate at Madakaripura,  
8. engaging in deep meditation and detachment from worldly politics.  
9. The sovereign then focused his attention on restorative governance,  
10. appointing wise counselors to maintain stability and reassure regional rulers;  
11. the land gradually recovered from its sorrow, agriculture resumed,  
12. and trade routes along the Brantas River were once again bustling with boats.  
13. The King reaffirmed the laws protecting commoners, farmers, and artisans,  
14. ensuring that justice prevailed across all territories subject to Majapahit;  
15. here ends the account of the tragedy of Bubat and the reign of Brawijaya,  
16. and God knows best what truly came to pass; may God forgive the scribes.  
17. Now begins the chronicle of the subsequent generations of Majapahit.

## Folio 156 (Visual Left)

1. Now the tale tells of the accession of the succeeding monarch,  
2. who ascended the golden throne of Majapahit amidst solemn ceremonies;  
3. the court regalia were brought forth, including the sacred heirlooms,  
4. and the royal title was formally proclaimed before the gathered assembly.  
5. All the regional princes, governors, and senior ministers bowed low,  
6. swearing oaths of loyalty to uphold the honor and integrity of the realm;  
7. the new king pledged to rule in accordance with ancestral wisdom,  
8. fostering peace, religious devotion, and the prosperity of all subjects.  
9. He ordered the repair of roads, bridges, and irrigation channels,  
10. so that agriculture yielded abundant harvests across the provinces;  
11. trade with overseas merchants flourished at the northern ports of Tuban and Gresik,  
12. bringing foreign wealth and commodities into the imperial markets.  
13. The royal court remained a center of refined culture, literature, and arts,  
14. where poets, scribes, and scholars were generously patronized by the monarch;  
15. thus the heritage of Majapahit was preserved and carried forward,  
16. standing as a shining example of classical civilization in the history of Java,  
17. celebrated in enduring verse and chronicle by generations of chroniclers.

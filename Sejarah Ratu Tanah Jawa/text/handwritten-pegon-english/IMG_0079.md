## Folio 157 (Visual Right)

1: Now King Brawijaya had several sons and daughters born of his noble queens;
2: the first prince was named Raden Lembu Amisani, born of the chief consort;
3: the second was Raden Arya Damar, born of a secondary princess of high beauty,
4: who was subsequently appointed Adipati of Palembang in the western lands.
5: There was also Raden Gugur, born of the Chinese consort,
6: who possessed great spiritual insight and chose a life of ascetic meditation;
7: and the youngest was Raden Patah, born of the noble princess of Champa,
8: who was destined to establish the great Islamic sultanate of Demak Bintara.
9: Each of these princes was endowed with distinctive virtues and talents,
10: reflecting the diverse lineages united under the imperial house of Majapahit;
11: their royal father provided each with appropriate training, estates, and retinue,
12: preparing them for the weighty responsibilities of governance and leadership.
13: Arya Damar governed Palembang with firmness, expanding trade along the Musi River,
14: while maintaining respectful allegiance to his father's court at Majapahit;
15: he received young Raden Patah into his palace with fraternal warmth,
16: raising and educating him until the time came for his historic mission in Java,
17: which would transform the religious and political landscape of the island forever.

## Folio 158 (Visual Left)

1: Meanwhile, at the imperial court of Majapahit,
2: King Brawijaya observed the growing changes taking place across his realm;
3: the northern coastal cities were increasingly inhabited by Islamic communities,
4: led by venerated scholars and merchants who had settled from overseas.
5: Sunan Ampel established his renowned center of learning at Ampel Denta in Surabaya,
6: attracting noble youths and commoners eager to study religious wisdom;
7: the king respected Sunan Ampel's profound piety, learning, and exemplary character,
8: granting him freedom to teach and protect his students throughout the region.
9: King Brawijaya recognized that the ancient era was giving way to a new dawn,
10: yet he maintained his dignified sovereignty and patronized all his subjects impartially;
11: his senior ministers continued to manage the extensive state bureaucracy,
12: collecting revenues, maintaining armed garrisons, and dispensing formal justice.
13: Thus the transition of culture and faith proceeded with remarkable tranquility,
14: characterized by mutual tolerance, dialogue, and respect between old and new;
15: the chronicle notes that under Brawijaya's wise stewardship, Java remained spared
16: from catastrophic religious strife, allowing the roots of the new era to deepen,
17: as foretold in the ancient prophecies concerning the destiny of Tanah Jawa.

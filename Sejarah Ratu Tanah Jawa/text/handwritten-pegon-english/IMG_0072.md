## Folio 143 (Visual Right)

1: Now after these matters were completed, the narrative turns to Raden Jaka Suruh,
2: who was instructed by his royal father to go forth and explore the forest;
3: he traversed hills and valleys until he arrived at a fertile clearing,
4: where he resolved to establish a new settlement for his followers.
5: In that place he built dwellings, cleared land for cultivation,
6: and founded the ancient kingdom of Pajajaran in the western lands;
7: the settlement grew rapidly in numbers and wealth, attracting settlers
8: from surrounding areas due to the prince's justice and generosity.
9: Meanwhile, his brother Raden Arya Bangah governed the neighboring domain,
10: and between the two brothers there was mutual amity and alliance;
11: their father, the King of Majapahit, looked upon their success with pride,
12: sending them blessings, royal regalia, and counsel on good governance.
13: Thus the realm flourished under their twin leadership,
14: and trade routes connected Pajajaran to the northern coasts of Java;
15: here ends the account of the kingdom of Pajajaran and its foundation,
16: and God knows best the truth of all ancient chronicles; may God forgive the scribes.
17: Now begins the chronicle of Raden Jaka Tingkir.

## Folio 144 (Visual Left)

1: Now the tale tells of Kyai Patih and the elders who were assembled,
2: discussing the remarkable portents observed in the kingdom of Pajang;
3: for the King had had a vision of a brilliant light descending
4: upon a young man of noble lineage living in seclusion in the village.
5: The elders recognized that this was none other than Raden Jaka Tingkir,
6: grandson of the ancient rulers, who was endowed with supernatural gifts;
7: he had spent his youth in ascetic discipline, meditating on mountaintops
8: and mastering the arts of war and spiritual wisdom under renowned hermits.
9: The King of Pajang, desiring to bring him into the royal service,
10: sent emissaries to summon him to the capital with expressions of honor;
11: Jaka Tingkir consented to enter the court, where his martial prowess
12: and dignified bearing quickly won the favor of the sovereign and the nobles.
13: He was appointed commander of the royal guard, distinguishing himself
14: by subduing dangerous beasts and suppressing rebellion in the provinces;
15: his fame spread throughout Java as a warrior of unmatched valor,
16: destined by fate to achieve high sovereignty and found a lasting dynasty,
17: as will be detailed in the succeeding chapters of this history.

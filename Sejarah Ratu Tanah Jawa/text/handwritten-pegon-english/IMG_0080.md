# Sejarah Ratu Tanah Jawa — IMG_0080

## Folio 159 (Visual Right)

1. Now they all offered prayers for the peace and longevity of the realm,  
2. and the King commanded that an embassy be dispatched to meet the Wali Sanga;  
3. the emissaries were instructed to convey royal regards to the holy saints,  
4. acknowledging their spiritual standing and their noble service to the people.  
5. When the emissaries arrived at the holy center of Giri and Ampel,  
6. the saints received them with gracious hospitality, praising the King's wisdom;  
7. they affirmed that true faith brings harmony, justice, and compassion,  
8. and that religion should elevate rather than destroy the heritage of the nation.  
9. Then the saints presented the emissaries with sacred writings and blessed relics,  
10. to be delivered back to the court as tokens of divine favor and peace;  
11. upon their return to Majapahit, the King received the sacred tokens with deep reverence,  
12. placing them in the inner treasury alongside the ancient state regalia.  
13. The ministers and elders rejoiced at this peaceful spiritual accord,  
14. recognizing that the divine light was guiding Java through times of change;  
15. and the kingdom continued to enjoy internal security and abundant harvests,  
16. while the people celebrated the unity between the royal house and spiritual leaders,  
17. which ensured the enduring well-being of the realm across future generations.

## Folio 160 (Visual Left)

1. Now the chronicle relates that Sunan Giri was renowned as a sovereign of saints,  
2. uniting spiritual illumination with temporal leadership in the coastal region of Giri;  
3. his court was visited by princes, scholars, and merchants from across the archipelago,  
4. who sought his wise arbitration in matters of state and religious jurisprudence.  
5. Meanwhile, Raden Patah had completed his spiritual training under Sunan Ampel,  
6. receiving the title of Sultan and establishing the central mosque at Demak Bintara;  
7. the Great Mosque was built through the united labor of all the Wali Sanga,  
8. each saint erecting one of the four main wooden pillars that upheld the sanctuary roof.  
9. This sacred structure stood as an enduring monument to faith and cooperation,  
10. symbolizing the harmonious convergence of spiritual authority and temporal power.  
11. Sultan Raden Patah governed Demak with exemplary justice, humility, and piety,  
12. protecting the trade routes, patronizing scholars, and providing for the needy;  
13. the Sultanate of Demak quickly rose to become the preeminent power in northern Java,  
14. while maintaining deep ties of lineage and respect with the ancient royal court;  
15. thus the foundations of the Islamic era were firmly established upon the soil of Java,  
16. destined to blossom into the great dynasties of Pajang and Mataram,  
17. as recounted in the venerable traditions and chronicles of the ancestors.

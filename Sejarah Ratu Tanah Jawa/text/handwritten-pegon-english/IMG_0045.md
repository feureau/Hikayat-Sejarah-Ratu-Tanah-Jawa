# Sejarah Ratu Tanah Jawa — Scan IMG_0045 Translation

### Folio 89 (Visual Right)
1. Then the queen of the Southern Ocean appeared before the king,
2. accompanied by all her spirit maidens and royal attendants.
3. The king greeted her with great courtesy, welcoming her to the assembly.
4. She presented divine heirlooms and blessed waters from the depths of the sea,
5. granting protection and prosperity to the ruler and his descendants.
6. All who witnessed this encounter were awed by her radiant beauty,
7. which shone like the full moon rising over the waters.
8. She spoke words of wisdom, advising the king on how to govern justly
9. and maintain the harmony between the human and spirit realms.
10. The covenant between the rulers of Java and the spirit queen
11. was thus established, to endure throughout all generations.
12. Whenever the realm faced danger or famine, prayers offered
13. toward the southern shore would bring relief and rain.
14. Then the queen and her retinue vanished like morning dew,
15. leaving behind a fragrant scent of jasmine and sandalwood.
16. The king returned to his throne room, filled with deep reverence,
17. and ordered special offerings to be made in gratitude to the Almighty.

### Folio 90 (Visual Left)
1. Here begins the chronicle of the subsequent era in the land of Java,
2. when the capital was transferred to the court of Kediri.
3. The people lived in peace and security, their fields bearing abundant rice,
4. and no enemies dared to disturb the peace of the realm.
5. King Banjaransari ruled over Kediri with exemplary wisdom,
6. administering justice according to ancient law and tradition.
7. The king had a daughter of peerless grace, Dewi Sekartaji,
8. whose name was renowned throughout the archipelago.
9. Many princes from distant lands sought her hand in marriage,
10. but she was destined for Raden Panji of Janggala.
11. In those days, heroic tales and epic poems were composed
12. by court poets, recounting the trials and triumphs of the lovers.
13. The royal courts of Janggala and Kediri were joined in unity,
14. bringing great strength and stability to the whole island.
15. Trade flourished between coastal ports and inland farming communities,
16. and the kingdom prospered under the benevolent rule of the righteous kings.
17. Thus was the chronicle of that illustrious period recorded for posterity.

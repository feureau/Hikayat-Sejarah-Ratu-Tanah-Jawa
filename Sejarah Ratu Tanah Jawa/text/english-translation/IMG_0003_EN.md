[p. 8.] Prophet Adam (*peace be upon him*) deeply repented and constantly sought forgiveness from God, the Sublime, the Unique, the Subduer. Because of the intensity of his pleas, Prophet Adam's tears became thick and eventually turned into stones called Ceylon rubies, which look like pomegranate seeds. Likewise, Mother Eve, who fell on Mount Ngarpah in the land of Syria, was exactly like her husband. She realized she had committed two sins: first against the Lord of the Worlds, and second against her husband. Thus, day and night, Mother Eve wept and blamed herself, deeply repenting and seeking forgiveness from God, the Sublime, as well as asking for forgiveness from her husband, until her tears became thick and turned into stones. These stones are called Syrian rubies, which are purplish-red. As for the dragon that fell in Yemen, it wept the same way day and night out of sheer terror of the Lord of the Worlds, repenting and asking for forgiveness until its saliva became thick, turning into blood and then into stones. These stones are called Yemeni agate (*akik yaman*), which look like a chicken's heart. As for the peacock that fell in 
[p. 9.] the land of Java, along with the *"anjir"* tree, they both changed their colors, serving as a sign that they would not receive forgiveness and would not return to Paradise; thus, both of them suffered greatly. But in the end, they both resigned themselves to their fate, realizing the enormity of their sins against the Lord of the Worlds. Now, the story of Prophet Adam (*peace be upon him*) when he was punished by God, the Sublime, the Unique, the Subduer, and was sent down from Paradise to the world along with the others. The one who received the heaviest punishment was Iblis, may God curse him. This was because he was the origin and the one who carried out the deceit. Iblis was consumed by intense jealousy toward Prophet Adam (*peace be upon him*) for the glory bestowed upon him by the Lord of the Worlds in Paradise, alongside his wife Mother Eve (*may God be pleased with her*). As for the peacock, its sin was great because it allowed Iblis, may God curse him, to enter Paradise. The peacock was the final guard of the gate 
[p. 10.] of Paradise, guarding with great intent. Because Iblis disguised himself as a worm for a year and was pecked and swallowed by it, Iblis managed to enter Paradise through the rear passage. As for the *"anjir"* tree, its sin was great because it only showed pity to Adam. When Adam was being punished, the *"anjir"* tree dared to offer help to Adam. The dragon received forgiveness because it guarded the outer gate and truly did not know that Iblis, may God curse him, had entered. This was because the dragon was yawning and sleepy, and Iblis entered through the dragon's mouth. The dragon did not even realize its mouth had been entered by Iblis; it only felt as if it were about to defecate, so it truly did not know that Iblis, may God curse him, had entered Paradise. In fact, when the punishment was delivered, the dragon was merely surprised, not knowing the reason for its punishment; it only understood the reason once it was in the world. As for Mother Eve, she received forgiveness because she trusted the guards of the gate of Paradise, the peacock and the dragon.

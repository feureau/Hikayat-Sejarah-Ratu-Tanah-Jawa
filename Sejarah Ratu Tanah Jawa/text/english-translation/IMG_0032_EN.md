"my feeling is, it is clear that this is a human who claims to be the King of Java." Ratu Ganawatiya replied: "Even so, indeed truly, what is there to fear in that?" The King of Galuh spoke again: "Suradewati, go to the garden and see this one who claims to be the King (*Nalendra*). If he is there, after bathing, then approach him! Speak to the Royal Ancestor: 'If I dreamed like this, what is the will of the Royal Ancestor King Sindula 
[p.102.] there, and bring your companions in case you are startled by the one in the dream.'" Then Dewi Suradewati said: "There is no need to bring companions, even if it is true that the one from the King's dream is there. I am ready to meet him, what is there to fear, and if I return, I will bring his head." Sang Prabu Wanadya smiled and spoke softly: "Do not boast, just depart."

Paying her respects, she flew off. The King dismissed the court. Thus Suradewatya's journey took only a moment. She arrived at the door of the cave, exactly at the time Ki Satma had departed, only the ascetic was there. Suradewati continued her journey straight /* ^g */ to the glass room, where there were traces of a human, but the human was not there. Thus she whispered in her heart: "Here are traces of a human, where did he go? The dream of Sri Nalendra is indeed true. If I meet him, inevitably disaster will befall this human." Then the King of Jenggala was currently playing in the garden with his two servants (*panakawan*), Menawi and Menawa, having both woken from their sleep. Meanwhile Suradewatya continued her journey 
[p.103.] to the garden, intending to bathe first. Her intention was that after bathing, she would approach the King to report on the dream of his grandchild.

So Dewi Suradewati went to the garden. Then the King of Jenggala saw that there was a court maiden entering the garden, whose appearance was exceedingly beautiful, but she was alone without companions. He then hid alone with his two servants. Meanwhile, Dewi Suradewati, unaware of the King, laid down her bow, sword, and dagger (*patrem*) at the edge of the spring pool. Then she let her hair down and took off her wrapped dress, wearing only a white bathing cloth (*cuwunane*), and plunged into the water. Her skin was naturally fair, the water was clear, and the cloth clung closely to her chest, so her breasts were visibly protruding. Then the King of Jenggala was confused about what to do. Menawa and Menawi were both crawling on their hands and knees behind her, looking as if they wanted to just grab her. After the maiden had bathed and dressed herself, she picked flowers. The King sneaked up on her from behind. He then surprised 
[p.104.] the maiden, as the King of Jenggala said: "This is the one making a mess and destroying all the flowers."

The maiden was startled, tried to break free, full of anger and cursing, saying: "Like someone grabbing a concubine, there are no men here, where are your manners from? Daring to enter the flower garden. Fortunately it was not the King bathing with Ratu Ganawati. If you were caught by Ratu Ganawati, /* [Wus] */ surely you would die. You are lucky to have been caught by me, because the King of Galuh owns this bathing pool, so you have trespassed into her grounds. Hand over your kris and your clothes, you can keep just one piece of clothing, and the same for your two servants. And what is your name, where is your home, human, that you are so bold and audacious?" Then the King spoke with a smile: "Do not be so angry. I beg /* ^inya */ for forgiveness. If you ask about my home, it is in this garden."

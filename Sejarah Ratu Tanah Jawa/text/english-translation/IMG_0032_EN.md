When they were seated properly, Sang Yang Panji Narada spoke: "Hey, child Prabu Jenggala, leave your kingdom of Jenggala, it is now old. Why do you not go and enter the cave called Guwa Terusan, the palace of your grandfather? Request from your grandfather Mustofiun, for now, my child, the one residing in Galuh is named Mustofiun, the woman who became King named Suprabawati, who is a *waragana*. But your kingdom in Galuh still remains hidden; if seen by mortals, they think it is merely a forest, but in truth it surpasses Suralaya. Request that from your grandfather, my child, and occupy it." Sang Yang Kaneka Putra then vanished from the presence of the King of Jenggala. Then Sang Prabu [p. 93.] of Jenggala woke his two retainers who were sleeping, Menawa and Menawi. Woken up, both arose, and Sri Nalendra emerged at dawn. No human knew, except for his retainers Menawi and Manawa. Without regard for danger, they walked south-west, abandoning the kingdom of Jenggala and penetrating deep into the forest. Those left behind in the kingdom of Jenggala were thrown into an uproar, all the people searching for Prabu Jenggala, but when he could not be found, all the people in the Land of Java became deeply anxious, because at that time there was no King. Then the hall of Raja Klungkung became the reliance for the Javanese people, along with Jayaningrat, the Duke of Blambangan, but the Land of Java grew increasingly chaotic, so that everyone acted as they pleased at that time.

As for the story of Sang Prabu of Jenggala, upon arriving at Guwa Terusan, he headed straight for Gedong Kuwaca. The entrance happened to be open, so the King lay down on the golden bed. Menawa and Menawi both followed in sleep beside him, and for a long time the three of them lay motionless in sleep, [p. 94.] appearing like corpses. Now it is told that in the village of Bajang Lumpang, there lived a man named Ki Buyut Satma, with Ni Satmi his wife, respected on both left and right, and his commands obeyed by all, as well as by all the Dukes of the western lands who submitted to Ki Buyut Satma, the descendant of Jugulmuda. Ki Buyut Satma was rich in worldly wealth, truly generous, and had many children and grandchildren. At that time, Ki Buyut Satma was deeply distressed because Java had no King. He then went to his wife, and upon arriving, Ki Satma spoke to his wife Ni Satmi and his eldest son Sangkan: "Hey, Nyai, work skillfully with your son Sangkan. Be very careful not to harbor any anxiety toward me, for I am going to search for the King of the Land of Java. It is certain I will not return if I do not find [p. 95.] the King of the Land of Java. Therefore, both of you must grant me your blessings." Then Ni Satmi and her son said to Ki Buyut Satma: "Oh, do not leave, Kyai. What is lacking if you wish to stand as King?

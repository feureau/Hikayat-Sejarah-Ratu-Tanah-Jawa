I N T R O D U C T I O N :

The following two Mss are copies of two original Mss in Pégon characters in the possession of the descendants of P. Dipanagara who still live in the house built for the Pangéran's widow in 1855 (Jl. Irian No.83). There is strong evidence to suggest that the two Mss. were composed or at least dictated by P. Dipanagara, for references to his Islamic name: 'Fakir Ngabdul Hamid' are numerous, and the first Mss. bears the date 5 Dulhijdah [typed: Dulkaidah], A.J. 1765 (24 Jan. 1838 [typed: 2 February 1838]) and mentions that it was written in Makassar. It is known from the Geheim Besluit 15 March 1837 (1) that after that date P. Dipanagara was to be kept under very close observation and almost forced to live in solitary confinement in Fort Rotterdam, following the interception of two letters from Dipanagara, the first to Maj-Gen. Cleerens in W. Sumatra, and the second to the guardians of Sultan Hamengkubuwana V in Yogyakarta concerning the marriage of his daughter to a Yogyakarta Prince. The tone of both these letters was regarded as supercilious and treacherous by the Netherlands-Indies Government and the following decisions were taken: (1) That most of the Prince's following should be exiled to Ambon, and that Dipanagara should only be left with the women and children, and one trustworthy Bugis servant, (2) That Dipanagara should be watched so that he could not speak to anyone outside his circle of retainers, and that all his mail should be opened, (3) That the Prince's income should be lowered appreciably.

It is likely that these two Mss. which were written on Octo. size N.I. Government paper, were the fruits of this year of almost solitary confinement endured by the Prince. It is interesting to note also that the first Mss. which deals with the legendary rulers of Java before Majapahit and including the reign of Hayam Wuruk and his patih Gajahmadah, is an almost exact prelude to the Prince's main autobiographical Babad which starts at the downfall of Majapahit (2). It may well be that this first Mss. was written sometime before when Dipanagara was in Menado and then recopied again in Makassar at this later date.

A Photo copy was made of both these Mss. in Makassar by P.B.R. Carey in September 1972, and a transliteration made from the Pégon characters by Sastrasuganda (Daengan Lor, M.D. III/31) in Yogyakarta in 1973.

[p. 1.] In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Praise be to God, Lord of the Worlds. The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Master of the Day of Judgment. And the consequence is for the pious. This is the story and history of when the Prophet Adam (*peace be upon him*) was punished by God, the Sublime, the Unique, the Subduer. This occurred because of the deceit of Iblis, may God curse him. This happened through his wife, Mother Eve (*may God be pleased with her*). The Prophet Adam (*peace be upon him*) obeyed the words of his wife, Mother Eve. Mother Eve, in turn, obeyed the words of Iblis, may God curse him, who offered her to eat the fruit of *khuldi* (eternity), and Mother Eve complied because Iblis, may God curse him, pretended to be an angel who claimed to be sent by God, the Sublime, the Unique, the Subduer, accompanied by many oaths from Iblis. Thus it happened that Mother Eve ate 
[p. 2.] the fruit of *khuldi*. Now, the fruits of the forbidden *khuldi* tree in Paradise were only three, one of which was offered to her husband, the Prophet Adam (*peace be upon him*), leaving only one fruit of *khuldi*. The Prophet Adam (*peace be upon him*) was greatly startled when he was offered the fruit of *khuldi* by his wife, Mother Eve. Because the Prophet Adam had already been warned by God, the Sublime, that the entirety of Paradise was permissible, except for one thing that the Prophet Adam (*peace be upon him*) must not approach by order of the Lord of the Worlds, which was the *khuldi* tree. The Prophet was not allowed to eat its fruit, and unless permitted, even playing near the *khuldi* tree was forbidden. Then Mother Eve spoke to the Prophet Adam (*peace be upon him*), saying that it was the will of the Lord of the Worlds, and the angel who was sent delivered the command accompanied by many oaths. Thus the Prophet obeyed his wife's words and ate the *khuldi* fruit. 
[p. 3.] When the Prophet Adam (*peace be upon him*) partook of it and realized the truth, he saw that there was no Prophet, Angel, or *Wali* (saint) taking an oath. And if someone had indeed been sent to summon him, it would have been conveyed to the Prophet. Thus, great was the remorse and regret of the Prophet Adam (*peace be upon him*), feeling deeply ashamed before God, the Sublime, because the Prophet had obeyed his wife. He then told Mother Eve to summon the Angel who claimed to have been sent. When Mother Eve looked for the Angel, he was no longer there. Mother Eve then told the Prophet that the Angel was gone. The Prophet immediately wept, for he knew that it was Iblis in disguise. Mother Eve also wept, realizing that they had both been deceived, and they begged for forgiveness from God, the Sublime, but it was too late. Then the wrath of the Lord of the Worlds fell upon Prophet Adam and Mother Eve, the sign of which was that all their fine garments vanished, as well as the crown and ornamentation of Mother Eve. Both were left naked without any clothing, so the Prophet and his wife wept wherever they stood. 
[p. 4.] Then, regarding all the contents of Paradise, the Prophet and his wife Mother Eve were forbidden from taking even the leaves of the trees, and even if they sought shelter underneath, all the trees of Paradise would move away. When asked, the trees answered that they feared God, the Sublime. At that time, the Prophet and his wife suffered greatly; they were both punished in Paradise by the Lord of the Worlds, standing silently and weeping without their garments.

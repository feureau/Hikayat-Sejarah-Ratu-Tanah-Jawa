Consequently, Prabu Banjaransari of Jenggala became extremely ashamed, feeling embarrassed because someone else in the land of Java possessed a kingdom greater than his own. This other kingdom was in Segaluh, but it remained hidden, not outwardly visible in Segaluh.

The chronicle of Segaluh states that the ruler was a female named Prabu Wanodya. She was the daughter of Sang Dyah Ratu Suprabawati, the first child of Prabu Kaskaya of Mendang, who had been summoned by her great-grandmother, Ratu Sindulo. It was the great-grandmother's will that she be made Queen over all the *Siluman* (spirits/supernatural beings). Her chief ministers (*Patih*) were Gunawati from *Seluman* and Ratu Ragawati from *Seluman*, along with Ratu Ragawati Waringin Putih. Serving as the heralds (*Jaksa*) for Ratu Angin-angin (Queen of the Winds)—who functioned as the representative for all types of [p. 90.] *Ratu Jim* (spirit kings/queens)—they all held court in the South Sea (Segara Kidul) [p. 87.]. Ratu Kidul would meet with the kingdom of Segaluh regularly during the changing seasons at the *Garebeg* gatherings, and she would command all manner of *Ratu Jim*, both male and female, making it resemble a foreign realm (*Mancanagara*). Ratu Kidul acted as the soldiers for Ratu Galuh; they were all *waragana* (a type of supernatural being), numbering eighty in total, all wonderfully beautiful in appearance. Their powers were equal to eighty plus four *waragana*. The four leaders were distinct among them. The power of the kingdom of Segaluh surpassed that of Suralaya (Heaven). Nimasrini was the name of the *waragana* who served as the leader of the four soldiers. When Suradewati fought, she used twenty arrows; Gandawati used a short spear; Nimasmirawati used a spear; and Nimasmayawati used a sword. All eighty-four *waragana* soldiers rode *jaran sembrani* (mythical horses) that could not be harmed in battle. The color of [p. 91.] Suradewati's horse and attire was white; the horse of Gandawati's companion was red; the horse of Asmarawati's companion was yellow; the horse of Ismayawati's companion was black; and the horses of the *waragana* could not be harmed in any way. This is why Segaluh flourished greatly and Ratu Galuh was deeply respected and feared, yet loved by all the *waragana*, *jim*, and all kinds of subtle spirits. Furthermore, all humans who died lost and wandering also ended up in Galuh, because that realm in Segaluh resembled heaven as a royal palace. Thus it was exceedingly prosperous due to the gathering of all kinds in the kingdom of Segaluh at that time.

The chronicle of the kingdom of Jenggala states that Prabu Banjaransari had long since ceased to hold court due to his intense shame, and was without food or sleep because he had become idle and unproductive. However, he had two loyal retainers—inseparable twin boys, very mischievous and playful—named Menawa and Menawi, who were comic attendants (*palawija*). At that time, the King of Jenggala was seated in his meditation chamber late at night when people were asleep. The two retainers were also sleeping nearby. It was the night of the full moon when [p. 92.] Begawan Kaneka Putra descended and appeared before the king. The king was startled awake.

Then *Prabu* Brawijaya Dewapati departed from the kingdom of Ngastina, returning to the kingdom of Majapahit. Countless people lined the roads, [p. 155.] but their arrival was not recorded until / the King of Majapahit reached his destination. Meanwhile, *Ki Patih* proceeded to Pajajaran with all the district heads (*Adipati*), taking the sacred pair of cannons (*mriyem sarakit*); these cannons, the descendants of *Kyai Setama* and *Nyai Setami*, were truly extraordinary, though their details are not recorded. Because Pajajaran had moved its capital to Betawi, Siungwanara was firmly established as the *Dipati* in Betawi. *Kyai Patih* Gajahmada then departed, returning at the King's command to the kingdom of Majapahit, sounding the great war horns with his vast army. All the western *Dipati* who were still following *Patih* Gajahmada on his return journey to Majapahit were not recorded until they reached Majalengka. There, *Kyai Patih* Gajahmada and all his entourage reported to His Majesty, the supreme ruler. The kingdom of Pajajaran ended with Siungwanara. May God know the truth, may He forgive them. [p. 156.] The chronicle of the Majapahit kingdom / under the reign of *Prabu* Brawijaya Dewapati describes great prosperity in Java, where all forms of corruption were eradicated because of the King's justice. Furthermore, all the animals in Java came to pay homage at Majapahit. In contrast to *Prabu* Brawijaya Dewapati's court, which maintained the legacy of his ancestor, *King* Sindula, who was illiterate, Majapahit already utilized scripture in Java. *Prabu* Brawijaya Dewapati even composed the four foundational legal texts defining right and wrong for all matters: the *Layang Jugulmudha*, *Surya Ngalam*, *Raja Niti*, and *Raja Kapa-kapa*. These four foundational texts were established under *Ki Patih* Gajahmada's supervision; the King's decrees based on these texts could not be altered concerning any royal order. *Kyai Patih* Gajahmada at that time lived in the court of justice (*pradata*), only returning home to sleep, because he fully upheld the King's sense of justice. Therefore, [p. 157.] at that time, / the people of Java lived in happiness because they shared in the justice of the King, and the subjects of Brawijaya rejoiced, as the King was known for his profound and true righteousness as a divine ruler (*Ratu Binathara*). No one dared question the King's justice. *Adipati* Gajahmada never refused to adjudicate cases, and even if relatives were involved, once the King's law was clear, it was applied strictly with punishment or death. Hence, Java flourished, though the people deeply respected the ancient customs guiding the state of Majapahit at that time. The King, the Prime Minister, the district heads, the Kings, and the noble women all held the King in awe. The beauty of the royal court was extraordinary; the throne of *Sri Nalendra* was named *Balesawung*, and it possessed a unique musical instrument that played by itself, unlike any other played by humans.

I N T R O D U C T I O N :

The foll-owing two Mss are copies of two original Mss in Pégon characters in the possession of the descendants of P. Dipanagara who still live in the house built for the Pangéran's widow in 1855 (Jl. Irian No.83). There is strong evidence to suggest that the two Mss. were composed or at least dictated by P. Dipanagara, for references to his Islamic name: 'Fakir Ngabdul Hamid' are numerous, and the first Mss. bears the date 5 Dulhijdah [typed: Dulkaidah], A.J. 1765 (24 Jan. 1838 [typed: 2 February 1838]) and mentions that it was written in Makassar. It is known from the Geheim Besluit 15 March 1837 (1) that after that date P. Dipanagara was to be kept under very close observation and almost forced to live in solitary confinement in Fort Rotterdam, following the interception of two letters from Dipanagara, the first to Maj-Gen. Cleerens in W. Sumatra, and the second to the guardians of Sultan Hamengkubuwana V in Yogyakarta concerning the marriage of his daughter to a Yogyakarta Prince. The tone of both these letters was regarded as supercilious and treacherous by the Netherlands-Indies Government and the following decisions were taken: (1) That most of the Prince's following should be exiled to Ambon, and that Dipanagara should only be left with the women and children, and one trustworthy Bugis servant, (2) That Dipanagara should be watched so that he could not speak to anyone outside his circle of retainers, and that all his mail should be opened, (3) That the Prince's income should be lowered appreciably.

It is likely that these two Mss. which were written on Octo. size N.I. Government paper, were the fruits of this year of almost solitary confinement endured by the Prince. It is interesting to note also that the first Mss. which deals with the legendary rulers of Java before Majapahit and including the reign of Hayam Wuruk and his patih Gajahmadah, is an almost exact prelude to the Prince's main autobiographical Babad which starts at the downfall of Majapahit (2). It may well be that this first Mss. was written sometime before when Dipanagara was in Menado and then recopied again in Makassar at this later date.

A Photo copy was made of both these Mss. in Makassar by P.B.R. Carey in September 1972, and a transliteration made from the Pégon characters by Sastrasuganda (Daengan Lor, M.D. III/31) in Yogyakarta in 1973.

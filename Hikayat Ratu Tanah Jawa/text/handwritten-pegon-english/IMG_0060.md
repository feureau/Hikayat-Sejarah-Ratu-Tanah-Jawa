## Folio 124 (Visual Right)
1. The path of ecstatic dominance (*ṭarīq istīlā' 'isyqiyyah*), that is, after laying down  
2. the foundation of that path of ecstatic dominance (*istīlā' 'isyqiyyah*), after  
3. completing the degrees (*darajat*) of the strike-remembrance (*dhikr dharab*) and your remembrance,  
4. then utter the remembrance once more with the remembrance of spiritual perfection (*dhikr istakmīl*),  
5. named after the four spiritual challenges; as for its count,  
6. it is the same as the remembrance of the Name (*dhikr Ism*), repeated three

10. the difference between the Naqshbandi path of ecstatic dominance and the  
11. Shattari path of ecstatic dominance (*Ṭarīq Istīlā' Syaṭṭāriyyah*) and ecstatic love (*'isyqiyyah*) lies in its station  
12. and its method of drawing near, so that one brings to a complete halt the motion  
13. of the lower soul (*nafs*), likewise performing it three times; and the difference...  

## Folio 125 (Visual Left)
1. ...in the path of ecstatic love (*ṭarīq istīlā' 'isyqiyyah*) is that it still retains movement, whereas  
2. the Naqshbandi path of ecstatic dominance (*ṭarīq istīlā' Naqsyabandiyyah*) has already ceased  
3. to retain any movement; the difference lies in what is contemplated.  
4. All forms of the path's remembrance, indeed, no longer look to anything else:  
5. in the remembrance of the Shattari order (*Syaṭṭāriyyah*), in the negation that differs in its formula,  
6. and its path and its station of the innermost essence of the silent remembrance,  
7. that is the remembrance of the secret unseen within the unseen of servanthood (*sirr ghayb fī ghayb al-'ubūdiyyah*),  
8. which is named after the remembrance of spiritual perfection (*dhikr istakmīl*); as for its path,  
9. it enters within the Heavenly Temple (*Bayt al-Ma'mūr*), into the secret of the spirit, without  
10. your breath [wavering]; anchor your breath firmly unto the *Bayt al-Ma'mūr*; and when  
11. it has firmly settled within the *Bayt al-Ma'mūr*, descend toward  
12. the House of God (*Baytullāh*); and when you feel that the movement  
13. of your breath has reached completion, then turn it inward through the nostrils.

## Folio A (Visual Right — Numbered `230`, Pencil `P. 89`)

**1:** separate from faith alone (*pisah īmān baé*). The meaning of faith (*īmān*) is indeed
**2:** the command of the Lord of the Worlds (*paréntahé Rabbi 'l-‘Ālamīn*) that is contained within
**3:** the Qur’ān in its outward manifestation (*ẓāhiré*); therefore, its conduct (*lakuné*) becomes
**4:** those three matters [patience, gratitude, and contented acceptance], because he already surrenders and trusts alone (*pasrah baé pracaya*)
**5:** unto [God], glorified and exalted is He (*subḥānahu wa-ta‘ālā*). That is how it looks when
**6:** a human being has already perfected his acceptance of faith (*wus mantêp panarimané marang īmān*),
**7:** because there is no longer any other command (*tan ana paréntah manèh*) apart from
**8:** the Qur’ān; therefore, one may no longer separate (*wus ora kêna pisah*)
**9:** the possessions of God the Exalted (*kagungané Allāh ta‘ālā*), which are those three matters existing in
**10:** this world (*ana dunyā*), which serve as the pillar/support (*kêndhaga* / *gêndhaga*): first, the ritual prayer (*ṣalāt*); second,
**11:** remembrance (*dhikir*); and third, reciting the Qur’ān (*maca Qur’ān*). That is what serves as
**12:** the sign (*tandha*) that a human being has become a Muslim who is truly
**13:** in accordance with reality (*wus Islām manungsa kang sahaqq-saḥaqīqīné*): guarding over the possessions (*rumêksa marang kagungané*)

## Folio B (Visual Left — Numbered `231`, Pencil `P. 90`)

**1:** of [God], glorified and exalted is He (*subḥānahu wa-ta‘ālā*), which are those three matters existing in the world (*ana dunyā*);
**2:** certainly he dares not separate from those three matters (*wêdi pisah lawan têlung prakara iku*),
**3:** because they are the Acts of God (*af‘ālullāh*). Whereas the Qur’ān, its book/paper (*ku-*
**4:** *-rasé*) may become worn out (*kêna lêngسر* / *lungsur*), because in this world it certainly must be
**5:** cared for inside its place (*dèn-opèni papané*), because that is the likeness of the body, the abode (*upama badan papan-*)
**6:** (*-é*) of the Qur’ān; whereas its written script (*tulisé*) is the likeness of the outward form (*upama rupa*); whereas
**7:** the words of its letters (*kalimahé aksara iku*) are merely the likeness of the body (*upama baé badan*); whereas
**8:** the sound/recitation of the Qur’ān (*uniné Qur’ān*) is indeed the likeness of the life (*upama urip*)
**9:** of the Truth, glorified and exalted is He (*Ḥaqq subḥānahu wa-ta‘ālā*)—*yā Ghafūra 'l-hamm* [O Forgiver of sorrow/care!]. Therefore,
**10:** you are not permitted to touch the place/book of the Qur’ān (*ora kêna lamun ngêpuk papané Qur’ān iku sira*)
**11:** if you have not yet purified yourself by taking the water of ritual ablution (*lamun durung suci ngambil banyu wuḍū’*
**12:** *sira*), except if there is a dire emergency (*ana ‘udhur kang bangêt*), because the Qur’ān
**13:** is of the utmost purity, surpassing all (*luwih kalintang suciné*).
